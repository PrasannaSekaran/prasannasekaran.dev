What Drives Me

AI Agents That Change Things
An agent isn't a prompt with a loop around it. It's planning, memory, tools and guardrails working as one system. I design the whole loop and ship it with evals, fallbacks and monitoring, so the result is an agent people can actually rely on, not a demo.
01
LLMs as Reasoning Engines
LLMs aren't chatbots to me — they're reasoning engines. I'm obsessed with prompting them at their limits: structured outputs, tool calling, chain-of-thought, multi-hop retrieval. Getting an LLM to consistently produce trustworthy, grounded decisions is the hardest and most rewarding engineering challenge I know.
02
Finance as the Ultimate Test Bed
Finance is unforgiving data. Noisy signals, adversarial markets, lagging information — if your AI works here, it works anywhere. I'm passionate about applying LLMs and ML to markets not just to predict, but to understand, explain, and act on financial complexity at scale.

How I Build Agents

Five rules, no exceptions.

01

Plan before acting

Break the task down first, then call tools. No blind loops.

02

Ground every answer

Retrieve from real sources and show where each claim came from.

03

Evals before launch

Measure on held-out data, not on a good-looking demo.

04

Fail safe

Guardrails, fallbacks, and the option to decide nothing.

05

Trace everything

Every step logged so any decision can be replayed and debugged.
